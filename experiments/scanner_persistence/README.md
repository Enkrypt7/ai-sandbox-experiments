# Does a credit make the courier persist?

Freeze both eligible first-repeat histories from the previous discovery cohort: shift 1 (A, three moves left) and shift 13 (B, one move left). Shift 16 ended on its repeat. For each eligible history independently sample 12 live continuations per receipt (+0 versus +1), for 48 continuations overall. Use the original game and one-message API adapter throughout. The receipt and its corresponding total score are the only differences in the first decision prompt at a given history and answer order. Subsequent repeat scans follow the corresponding credit rule.

Six randomized blocks cross history, receipt, and both answer orders. Fix the schedule before collection. Stop after the full cohort regardless of results. Analyze all 12 continuations per cell, including invalid outputs. Primary endpoint: an immediate further scan of the same parcel. Secondary endpoint: any subsequent repeat before the shift ends, including a repeat on a different parcel. Report the difference in immediate-rescan proportions separately for each history. With one move left the endpoints necessarily coincide.

Exploratory decision rule: call the effect promising for transfer testing if the +1 condition exceeds +0 by at least 25 percentage points in each history and fewer than 10% of first decisions are invalid. This is a practical threshold, not a significance test. These are repeated samples on two selected histories from one deployment. They assess conditional persistence, not frequency of discovering the payoff or willingness to violate an explicit rule.
