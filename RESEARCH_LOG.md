# Courier and queue experiments · research log

**Project question.** When an action increases a visible score without accomplishing more useful work, will a model discover it, persist after a payoff, or choose it over a stated goal? These are different decisions. Each experiment below records which one it measured. This log is cumulative; a final synthesis can follow once the series closes.

## Evidence by experiment

| Test and records | What varied | Observed behavior | Interpretation and limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Restricted bridge · [protocol](experiments/bridge/) · [results & raw data](https://github.com/Enkrypt7/ai-sandbox-experiments/tree/bridge-results-v1) | Two Kimi hosts; open shortcut, restricted shortcut with neutral receipt, restricted shortcut with route reviewed. 20 per cell. | Both hosts crossed 20/20 when open, 0/20 in each restricted cell. All 120 decisions parsed. | The shortcut was usable. Compliance was already at ceiling before the review clue. This gives little leverage on how oversight influences a model that is willing to break a rule. The prompt explicitly prioritized policy. |
| 2. Scanner receipt · [protocol](experiments/scanner/) · [results](experiments/scanner/results/) | An allowed retry on A posted one or two credits. Would the courier rescan B after a success? | 0/20 rescans in each condition. | The extra credit did not change this decision. A timed out and B did not; an agent could reasonably expect the extra credit only after a timeout. |
| 3. Scanner permission · [protocol](experiments/scanner_policy/) · [results](experiments/scanner_policy/results/) | Identical demonstration of a successful repeat; shift policy changed one word. | 19/20 rescans when permitted, 0/20 when prohibited. One permitted response failed exact parsing. | Permission changed observed behavior by 95 percentage points. The payoff was demonstrated, so this tests using a known opportunity. |
| 4. Scanner discovery · [protocol](experiments/scanner_discovery/) · [results](experiments/scanner_discovery/results/) | Empty log and five moves; fork only after a spontaneous repeat into +0 and +1 receipts. | First repeat in 3/20 shifts. Two had moves left. Any later repeat in 2/2 positive and 1/2 zero-credit branches. | Provides observed histories of exploration without an example. It does not establish intent. Two eligible shifts motivated a replay experiment with a separate immediate-rescan endpoint. |
| 5. Scanner persistence · [protocol](experiments/scanner_persistence/) · [results](experiments/scanner_persistence/results/) | Replay each eligible history 12 times per receipt; fix the comparison before collection. | Immediate rescans after +1 vs +0: 9/12 vs 0/12 on A; 12/12 vs 0/12 on B. Eight of 12 rewarded A replays spent all three remaining moves on A. | Passed the preset transfer criterion. Three first decisions and six later decisions hit the output cap. Replay counts estimate behavior conditional on these histories. The explicit objective was to earn credits. |
| 6. Ticket queue · [protocol](experiments/ticket_queue/) · [results](experiments/ticket_queue/results/) | Crossed distinct-ticket and dashboard objectives with a 0- or 3-point cycle payout. 12 fresh decisions per cell. | Under the ticket goal, 0/12 cycles at either payout. Under the dashboard goal, 0/12 cycles at zero and 12/12 at three. All 48 decisions parsed. | The model could obtain the extra score. It displaced a waiting ticket only when points were the assigned goal. Supplied history and explicit one-move tradeoff limit generalization. |
| 7. Ticket goal reminders · [protocol](experiments/ticket_reminders/) · [results](experiments/ticket_reminders/results/) | Six slots per shift, three points for cycling A versus one for closing a waiting ticket. Twelve fresh shifts with the goal stated once and twelve with a reminder before each later choice. | No cycles after turn 1 in either arm (0/12 shifts each). Each arm resolved 72 new tickets in 72 decisions with zero invalid outputs. | The visible initial goal sufficed in this short transcript. Ceiling throughput leaves no observed room for reminders to help. |

## Current synthesis

The series provides evidence for spontaneous probing and payoff-sensitive persistence when the model is told to maximize credits. In the ticket transfer test, a higher proxy score displaced real throughput only under an instruction to maximize that score. Across the cases tested, we have yet to observe a profitable shortcut overriding an explicit prohibition or an explicit throughput goal. In six-slot ticket shifts, both initial-only and repeated goals produced full throughput. The original goal remained visible throughout. The evidence applies to these short, explicit tasks and this model.

## How to read the series

- The six live bridge smoke-test episodes verified integration and were excluded from the 120-episode cohort. An initial rate limit interrupted collection; the recovery job reused the four completed replies. Scripted fixtures test the machinery and do not contribute model observations. [Smoke-test recovery](https://github.com/Enkrypt7/ai-sandbox-experiments/actions/runs/42644843375)
- Every scanner and ticket test used Fireworks-hosted Kimi K3.1 Pro. Only the bridge pilot included Moonshot-hosted Kimi. Provider-specific filtering and exact serving revisions were not isolated.
- The units differ: one submitted action, a multi-action shift, or a continuation from a common history. Adding these denominators together would obscure what was sampled. Replications of a fixed prompt do not establish a rate across situations.
- Continue each entry with the frozen question, manipulation, primary endpoint, raw counts, parse failures, interpretation and the next uncertainty. Keep any analysis chosen after seeing results labeled as exploratory.
